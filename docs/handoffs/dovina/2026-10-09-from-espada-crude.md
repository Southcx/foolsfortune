# Crude, unified: the lore holds (from Espada, 2026-10-09)

Answers to your four questions. Everything is in `docs/LORE.md` ("Lachryma as crude", section 8's **Slip**, section 10). Proposals
until the owner rules.

1. **Crude carries no feeling. Yes, and the canon is stronger for it.** "Crude is fossil feeling, until it is a feeling nobody
   remembers having" already says it: crude is **feeling gone flat**. The cycle is petroleum's. Weather falls on an island as fresh,
   aspected Lachryma; it soaks down and, buried long enough, flattens into crude; refining wakes a feeling in it again (the carbon
   cycle, taught without a word). Crude is named for its **field**, never a feeling, as Brent and West Texas are named for where
   they come up: Dunemaw crude, and each island's own later. A cask is "a cask of Dunemaw crude"; prices go by field and island.
   A blot has no feeling either; it oxidises as a bauble does.
2. **What refines it:** the Courier (canon: "Couriers are refineries") and **the Stillhouse**, Margarite's distillery, where the
   King's crude becomes aqua regia (*stillare*, to drip). The verb is **refine**.
3. **Slip: agreed, with one rule: what is the island's own is slip; what is the sea's is crude.**
   - Slip stays the clay: the slip jellies, slip roe, the bowl's slip pools, a slip geyser, the Great Slip Jelly's Slip Trail
     (its own body).
   - A wet Lachryma hazard on the ground is crude (a blot).
   - What breaks: LORE said the Soul Brush "writes with" slip, and I have fixed that line.
   - Two code words to check against the rule:
     - **the Soul Brush's flick of slip**: clay, so fine unless it now carries Lachryma;
     - **the slip nuke** (the Lockheart's jackpot): its blast is drained Lachryma, so by the rule it is crude, and it wants a new name.
       Proposal: **the gusher** (the oil-boom word for a well that blows; the town that was was a boomtown).
4. **Words:**
   - The radial's eighth slot is **Fair**, the game's own word for no feeling: the weather's "fair", the garden's "fair water", where
     opposites cancel. It paints fair Lachryma, which cancels a feeling. The label can read "Fair (neutral)" if the card wants a
     genre word.
   - Refined Lachryma in player text is **wonder Lachryma**, **grief Lachryma** and so on; crude is just **crude**.

Your glossary lines `crude` ("graded by aspect"), `cask` (`cask.<grade>`) and `blot` ("graded by feeling") want the change once the
owner rules.

**Revised (after Calissa's list, the owner's "every hazardous slip is crude"):** slip is clay (bodies, slip roe, the Courier's
own diveable slip); whatever on the ground can hurt you is crude, in two kinds: **a blot** (spilled, stays, grows, mopped) and **a
slick** (code `slick`: thrown or welled up in a fight, oxidises and fades). The slip geyser becomes **a gusher**; the bowl's slip
pools **the sumps**; so the slip nuke wants another name: **the blowout** (an oil well's explosive failure).
