# From Espada: where the numerals are worn (South, 2026-10-29; LORE.md, "The numerals")

South: "The dice absolutely should showcase your numeral creations. You guys don't give yourselves enough credit and think too small.
We can display numbers in arabic numerals as a part of other UI elements or as projected textures on a quad."

**The rule.** Things wear neuralese numerals. Wherever a player acts on a number, it is also said in Arabic, in the UI or on a quad
projected over the thing. The prior art is mahjong's character suit: players learn its Chinese numerals at the table, with no lesson.

In my order of worth (yours to draw and to order):
1. **Every die.**
   - The stones' seven: `vesseldice.js`, `palmdie.js` and the fingers' dice.
   - The flee's d20: `duelview/flee.js`.
   - Raku's house set, in `bazaarfaces.js`'s atlas. The clay folk had the script from the ancients.
   - A face's numeral sits on its own short bus, and the bus shows which way is up, so 6 and 9 can't be confused.
   - The rolled number is said in Arabic beside it: the flee's plate, a seat plate, or a quad over the settled die.
   - A d20 face needs two digits, so `numeralOf(20)` gives a pair and a ring.
2. **The Sight's wadokei hour ring:** the hours in numerals.
3. **The Lachrimeter's scale plate:** the graduations.
4. **The Major Arcana:** a second index beside the Roman one, on the cards, the reading view and the house deck.
5. **A caster shell's casing:** TYPE-nn etched on the brass.
6. **Posts and doors:**
   - the course's stations;
   - the Raceway's gates;
   - the Great Dunemaw's floors.
7. **The Comb:** the Seal Book's imprint numbers and the cradles' collars (my note of earlier today).

Wherever a number is read in play (a roll, a count, a price), the Arabic readout stays. Delete once drawn.
