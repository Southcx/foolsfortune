My read of A, B and C

  • A, the spread, is the one look. It is the only one where the new thing (the pair) is the hero. The title is Fool's Fortune, the trumps are the pairs, and the Courier is the Fool reading them. It also matches the house deck at Raku's tables and the Veritome's cards, so the card stock is already ours.
  • Where I'd push A:
    • The reading lies on a clay altar, not a felt table. The cards are glazed slips; the arcana's edge is the kintsugi gold the crossing's parts wear.
    • A tool's fittings sit on its own card as glazed tiles (the lure, the coffin, the keyring), so a tool and what's fitted to it read as one thing.
    • From C, keep the summoning: P slows the world and the window stands in the air before the Courier, not a pause page. Drop C's ring of 56; the pocket stays a flat tray, because a ring that size can't be read.
    • The sub lies crossed, as the Celtic Cross's second card, but its words are set level in game (here they turn with it): only the card's body crosses.
    • The refusal is your seal pressed in vermilion over the position: the hanko says no in the game's own hand, not a grey button.
  • What I'd refuse:
    • B, the attaché case, whole. It breaks the owner's one-thing-a-slot, and RE4's leather and foam is a look from another house. Tetris-ing a coffin next to a cask fights the cards.
    • C's orbiting tools at the waist. Five things circling the Courier during a slowed world is busy, and the pair (the point) gets lost among them.
    • Stock card frames. Every card is drawn in the Clay dress; no rounded-rectangle default anywhere.
  • Placeholders here: the emoji icons stand in for the pixel art (1x, palette-swapped, the UI icons' hand); the specials' opening lines are mine, Dovina's to set.
