# The Emocean

The Emocean is the sea between islands. You cross it in your ship, one hop at a time, by drafting a route on a sea chart and then flying
it as a rail shooter (a shooter where the ship rides a path and you dodge and aim). You care because it is the way to other islands, the
way crude Lachryma (crude) is hauled for profit, and a place where skill earns ranks.

A crossing pays no cubes. It costs fuel and risks cargo. Its reward is the price at the other end, a rank, and your records.

## Setting out: the pier

- Go to the end of a jetty and press **F**. The pier page opens. Anagami's jetty is on the shore. Margarite's is at its dock.
- The page lists the islands. Pick one to open its sea chart. An island not yet found is greyed out and says why.
- Below that you choose your ship and your mounts (the tools you carry at sea).
- Casting off pays the fuel in cubes, from your purse.

A hop's fuel is about 1.5 cubes for every step of distance, times the ship's fill factor. Anagami to Margarite is 4 steps. King's island
to Queen's island is 8 steps, the longest.

## Choosing your ship

A ship is a trade. Light ships are quick and can go anywhere. Heavy ships haul a lot but only sail a route someone has already charted
(you need a rutter for it, see below).

| Ship | Hits it bears | Mounts | Hold (casks) | Fuel (measures) | Speed | Dives | Sails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sloop | 6 | 2 | 8 | 7.5 | x1.2 | yes | any route |
| Frigate | 8 | 3 | 6 | 8.5 | x1.1 | yes | any route |
| Destroyer | 5 | 2 | 2 | 7 | x1.3 | yes | any route |
| Galleon | 12 | 1 | 40 | 13 | x0.8 | no | charted only |
| Tanker | 14 | 1 | 60 | 14 | x0.7 | no | charted only |

- **Hits it bears** is how many hits the ship takes before the leg is failed.
- The galleon carries refined cubes and goods. The tanker carries only crude, and is double-hulled so less spills.
- Only crude and goods go on a sloop. A frigate and a destroyer carry goods only. A ship refuses cargo it cannot carry or a hold that is over.
- The galleon and tanker cannot take the Umbral form (see Q below). They lock on at most 4 targets, not 8.

## Choosing your mounts

Mounts are tools you wear on the belt, carried on the ship. The Blaster (the psygun) is always aboard. The other tools fill your ship's
mount slots, fired on keys **1** to **3** in the order you pick.

| Mount | Tool | What it does | Numbers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blaster** | the psygun | Fire with LMB; hold RMB to lock on | always mounted; 3 energy a lock |
| **Absorb** | the Soul Brush | Eats enemy shots of your colour | 50 degrees, 9 m; 4 energy |
| **Bomb** (the Crucibelle's toll) | the Crucibelle | Clears every shot around you | 10 m (14 m on the beat); 3 a crossing |
| **Vacuum** (the Lockheart's gulp) | the Lockheart | Sucks in shots and small fish; refills energy | 35 degrees, 8 m; 6 s cooldown |
| **Flash** (the Veritome's plate) | the Veritome | Opens weak points for a few seconds | 6 energy; 6 s cooldown |
| **Grapple** (the Sondelass's hook) | the Sondelass | Pulls loot in; yanks boarders off | 16 m; 1.5 s cooldown |
| **Radar** (the Dreamvane's vane) | the Dreamvane | Warns you of attacks earlier | passive |

A tool must be worn to be mounted. Energy is your Lachryma pool.

## Drafting a route: the sea chart

Choosing an island opens its sea chart. It is a grid of waypoints, laid out in columns from your island to the far one. Lines join each
waypoint to the next column. You pick one waypoint in each column. Your picks are the route, and each waypoint you pass is a leg you fly.

- The chart is the same for everyone on a route for a whole game day, then it changes. Learn it, and come back tomorrow for a new one.
- A route has 3 to 6 columns. The longer and wilder the route, the more columns.
- A long crossing has 1 to 3 set pieces (see below).

### What waypoints are

| Type | Kind | What you find |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shoal | threat | a school of fish that rises and strikes |
| Wreckers | threat | pirates; more likely the more casks you carry |
| Eyewall | threat | a tunnel through a psychic storm |
| Graveyard | threat | sunken hulls below, ghost ships above |
| Calm | haven | a rest: mend the hull, or read the sea; fuel is sold at the buoy |
| Encounter | haven | a short scene that ends in a choice |
| Maelstrom | boss | a whirlpool; one a chart at most |
| Bounty | boss | a posted stray to hunt (when Letty Marque has posted one) |
| Leviathan | boss | Old Nobody, in the last columns, rare |

### Reading the sea: portents and the reckoning

You do not see the whole chart. A **portent** is a hint of a waypoint. The next waypoint is always exact. Further out, a portent shows
a short list of two or three possible types, then only a class (threat, haven or boss), then a dim star. What a portent shows is always true.

How far you see depends on your **reckoning**, from 0 to 100% for a route and a game day. You raise it by surveying the sea at the pier
(a Divination act) and by reading waves as you sail. Divination levels also help. Weather matters too: fog, rain and the pall (dread's weather) hide the sea, and wonder's weather helps.

At sea, a higher reckoning marks each wave's lane ahead of time, up to 3 real seconds early (two bars). It buys knowledge, never numbers.
A reckoning of 60% or more at the pier also opens a locked island for good.

### The squall

One waypoint on a chart (two on a long one) may carry a **squall**. It is a threat a class stronger, with thicker shot patterns. A
squall is never on a waypoint every route must cross, so you always may go around. Clear it and that leg's score is worth 1.25 times.

### Following seas

Every waypoint has a feeling. It sets the damage type of its foes and whether its shots are mostly Astral or Umbral. The feeling you
absorb on a leg is your draught. If your draught's damage type trumps the next waypoint's foes, you have a **following sea**. You can
draft a route so that each leg's draught trumps the next. Crude cargo spills 1.5 times as often at a waypoint of its opposite feeling,
and 0.75 times as often at its own.

## The trip's pressures

| Pressure | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Hull** | Hits you take stay with you from leg to leg. Only a haven mends them. |
| **Bunker** | Your fuel, in measures. It is filled at the pier and at a calm's buoy. |
| **Burn** | Each waypoint burns its type's measures times your ship's burn. |
| **Adrift** | Too little fuel for any way on: the current carries you. You burn nothing, and legs top out at rank C. |
| **Heaving to** | At a calm, choose: mend half the hull, or reckon the sea (portents ahead a quarter sharper). |
| **High water** | The best score on one route's chart for one game day. |

Burn by waypoint type (measures): shoal 1, graveyard 1, wreckers 1.25, bounty 1.25, eyewall 1.5, maelstrom 1.5, leviathan 1.5,
encounter 0.75, calm 0.5. A squall burns a quarter more. A diagonal step burns 1.15 times. Sloop burn is x1; frigate x1.1; destroyer
x0.9; galleon x1.5; tanker x1.8.

Every chart has a lane a full bunker can sail. The calms refill it. Wild lanes can be a fuel plan as well as a fight.

## Flying a leg: the rail shooter

A crossing is built to music: 100 bars of 1.5 real seconds each, 150 real seconds for one set piece. Everything happens on the beat.
The camera swings between five views in about one bar, and nothing new enters during a swing.

| View | Look | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chase | behind the ship | the mouse |
| Above | straight down on the crude | the gun fires along the scroll |
| Side | a 2D duel | the gun fires along the scroll |
| Free | the ship on a rail, loose reticle | the mouse |
| Astern | looking back at what chases you | the mouse |

### Controls at sea

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| WASD | move in the view's plane |
| Mouse | aim (in chase, free and astern) |
| LMB held | **Blaster**: full auto, a shot each sixteenth note |
| RMB held, release | **Lock-on**: mark up to 8 targets, release to fire a homing lance at each |
| E | roll: dodge (limited charges) |
| V | **Parry**: a 0.25 s window; answers an outlined shot by sending it back |
| Q | switch form: Astral (above the Emocean) or Umbral (below it) |
| R | **Surge**: let go the absorbed Lachryma (see below) |
| Shift / C | boost and brake along the rail |
| 1 to 3 | fire your mounts |

### Astral and Umbral

The ship has two forms. Q crosses the surface in a half bar.

| Form | Speed | Shot | Locks | Foes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Astral | x1.2, wide box | a spread of three | 8 | the sky's threats |
| Umbral | x0.85, tight box | one heavy shot (x2.5) | 4 | what swims below |

- Foe shots come in two kinds, astral (bright) and umbral (dark). **Absorb** a shot of your form's kind: it is drunk, gives +1
  Lachryma and fills the Surge. The other kind hurts.
- Your shots strike foes of the other kind. Some weak points open only in one world.
- Outlined shots wear a mark. Only a parry answers them: it sends the shot back.
- **Surge**: each absorbed shot fills it by 4, to 100. At full, R fires a lance at everything on screen and gives a half bar of safety.
  It costs you your chain.
- The tanker and galleon cannot dive. They stay Astral and dodge the Umbral shots.

### The legs' set pieces

| Set piece | The idea |
| --- | --- |
| **The shoal** | A boid school boils up, balls you in, then strikes in pulses. Shoot or lock its caller to scatter it. |
| **The Wreckers** | A brig with a false light. Parry her bow shots, cut her rigging, hole her hull. Boarders steal casks. |
| **Old Nobody** | The rogue Leviathan. Shut its four gills and strike the throat before bar 96 to fell it; else it sounds and is gone. |
| **The eyewall** | A run through a psychic storm. Folding geometry, sweeping beams, few foes. |
| **The graveyard** | Sunken hulls below, ghosts above, mines. You fight in both worlds. |
| **The maelstrom** | A whirlpool arena. **Charybdis** rises into the Astral and dives into the Umbral. |

Details of three:

- **Wreckers**: the chance a crossing meets pirates is 10% plus 6% a cask aboard (60% at most). Boarders every 4 bars take a cask each
  if they stand on your deck a bar.
- **Old Nobody** comes about once in every 14 crossings on the Margarite run (fewer on wilder routes), and is certain by the last one. Driven off it gives 1 shard of Lachrymite;
  felled, 3.
- **Charybdis** is hurt only from the world it is in: Astral shots when risen, Umbral when dived. At the surface it takes double. It has
  480 hit points. A ship that cannot dive can hurt it only risen or at the surface. Felled, it gives 2 shards of Lachrymite.

### Encounters

An encounter is a haven. It mends the hull by 3, then shows a scene ending in a choice. Each one you have not met is more likely next
time.

| Encounter | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| Ghost convoy | follow (next two waypoints shown exactly) or loot (2 casks, Wreckers drawn to you) |
| Letty's cutter | take a bounty ahead, or sell a rutter at 1.25 times its worth |
| Light whale | listen (reckoning up a quarter) or follow it down (a richer hidden Umbral leg; diving ships only) |
| Castaway | rescue (1 measure of fuel, an extra mount for a leg) or leave |
| Purser's barge | trade casks (sell at 0.85, buy at 1.15 of posted) or buy today's rutter |
| Mirror sea | race your best run's ghost: win, and the leg's rank goes up a letter |
| Drift bottle | an ostracon word, or one waypoint made exact |

## Scoring

A crossing pays points, not cubes. Points come from:

| Source | Points |
| --- | --- |
| A down by class (Guppy to Leviathan) | 100, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000 |
| Point blank (within 4 m) | x2 |
| Killed with its own shot sent back | x3 |
| A chain of three downs of one feeling | 100, then doubling each, up to 25,600 |
| A lock-on volley, all targets downed | 50, doubling for each lock after the first (8 locks: 6,400) |
| An absorb | 10 |
| Each hit the ship could still bear, at the end | 1,000 |

Your **rank** is your score against par for the set piece: S at par, A at half, B at a quarter, C at an eighth, else D. Par is 102,000
for the shoal, 37,000 for the Wreckers and 60,500 for Old Nobody. The **medal** is for passing with four in five of what came at you
downed. A drafted route also keeps your high water.

### Continues

When the ship has borne all it can, you may pay for a **continue**. The ship is mended whole and flies on. The cost is two real minutes
of the aim plus the fuel back to your last Shrine's island, doubled for each continue in one crossing. A continued run never ranks above C
and never medals. If you decline, the ship breaks up and you are made whole at your last Shrine, losing a quarter of your cargo.

## Cargo and rutters

- **Casks** of crude are carried in the Pneuka Box. A ship's hold limits how many may cross.
- A failed crossing loses a quarter of each grade of crude. A cask may spill whole as well, more often with a bigger load.
- You sell crude to the **Purser** at Margarite's dock at a posted price. Crude from Entropolis sells dearest at Margarite.
- A **rutter** is a drafted route sailed to its end. It is a map of that sea chart, worth cubes, and is sold or traded. Its worth
  halves each game day. Heavy hulls need one to sail. A rutter can also record a squall you cleared, which makes it worth more.
