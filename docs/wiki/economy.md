# Things and money

Cubes are the only money in the game. You earn them by doing things well, and you spend them on chests, goods, looks and the Spirit
Garden. This page says where cubes come from, where they go, and what each thing is worth.

## The aim

The game is tuned so that ordinary play earns about **8 cubes a real minute, 480 cubes a real hour**. Every price is set as a number of
minutes of play. No single livelihood is meant to pay more than about 1.5 times the aim. Only luck (a prismatic chest) breaks that.

## Your Pneuka Box

Press **P** to open the Pneuka Box: 56 slots, one thing to a slot. Keys stack to 99 in one slot. On the right is what you wear: the lure
on your line, each tool's fittings, the psygun's chambers and the five tools. Left click does the obvious thing. Right click lists
everything you can do. Drag to swap. If the box is full, a new thing falls at your feet.

## Cubes

A Lachryma cube is a small black cube with an oil-slick sheen. You pick them up where they fall. The game never shows a number
floating over them: the log says what you got.

### What earns cubes

| Source | What you do | Pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slip jelly | burst one yourself | 6 cubes (plus any it swallowed) |
| Zandatsu | cut a stunned creature into pieces | 3 cubes per core |
| Crystal | break one open in the Dunes | 2 + 4.5 per size; x0.6 (dense) or x0.5 (fragile) if struck plainly; x2 (dense) or x6 (fragile) if tuned by ear |
| Chest | open one | 4 to 700 cubes by tier (table below) |
| Fish | sell to Old Grog | 10 / 18 / 40 / 90 / 400 cubes by fish tier 1 to 5 |
| Curios | sell to Raku | 16 / 32 / 70 / 120 / 220 cubes by tier |
| Spare Veritome cards | condense one | 5 cubes (H) up to 320 (SS) |
| Lockheart | a CUBES outcome | 12 cubes per unit of power |
| Busking | play a song at a pier's mat | up to 1.5x the song's length in minutes of play, by your accuracy |
| Spirit Garden | collect a mastered encounter's dividend | 5% of its hourly rate, 8 game days at most |
| Wells | pots and artifacts you carry home | by depth; see [The Great Dunemaw](great-dunemaw.md) |

Selling to the folk pays a little better than condensing a card, on purpose: a card is for collecting first.

### What spends cubes

| Drain | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| The Tithe | 48 cubes a pull |
| A firing at the kiln | 16 cubes |
| Mending cracks at the kiln | up to 12 cubes (fewer for fewer cracks) |
| Bought looks at the kiln | 80 / 200 / 480 cubes (stoneware, porcelain, the Court) |
| Goods from the folk | see "The shops" |
| Fuel for a crossing | by distance; see [The Emocean](emocean.md) |
| The Spirit Garden | the long sink: planetoids 480 to 3,840 cubes, beds and slots; see [The Spirit Garden](spirit-garden.md) |

Not built yet: commissions (a hunt for a Figment by class), throwing pots for pay. Their pay rules exist in the economy table and
nothing in play uses them.

## Chests

A chest comes in five tiers. A chest is its tier from the moment you see it. Each holds cubes, and sometimes a curio.

| Tier | Cubes | Curio at least once in | Treasury reopens after |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Common | 4 to 9 | 5 chests | 4 real minutes |
| Fine | 14 to 26 | 4 chests | 15 real minutes |
| Rare | 40 to 70 | 5 chests | 40 real minutes |
| Epic | 120 to 200 | 2 chests | 80 real minutes |
| Prismatic | 400 to 700 | every chest | 160 real minutes |

Opening is a short show. A prismatic chest is a seven-second blacklight rave. A legendary fish catch drops an epic chest at your feet.

### Curios

There are twenty chest curios, four to a tier. A curio has no use: it is a collection and something to sell. The curio is a "deck": it
turns up at least once in the number of chests above, and each curio of a tier comes before any comes twice. If you already hold the
curio, it turns into cubes: 4 / 12 / 30 / 80 / 240 by tier. A twenty-first curio, the Crown of the Dunemaw, comes only from the Great
Slip Jelly.

### The Tithe

The Tithe is a console in the Weir's treasury, in the Dunes. You pay **48 cubes** and a sealed chest lands on the dais. Its tier is
rolled when you pay and shown only when you open it. The odds are public:

| Tier | Base weight | As you meet it (pity counted) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Common | 60% | 56.8% |
| Fine | 26% | 24.3% |
| Rare | 10.5% | 13.8% |
| Epic | 3% | 3.8% |
| Prismatic | 0.5% | 1.3% |

**Pity** turns a run of bad pulls into a sure thing:

| After this many pulls without it | The next pull is |
| ---: | --- |
| 10 | rare or better |
| 40 | epic or better (12% of the time prismatic) |
| 100 | prismatic |

The console shows the counters as rows of lamps, and the Codex shows the odds and your counts. The Tithe gives back roughly three
quarters of what it takes in cubes, so it is a gamble for curios, not a living. Gambling is never meant to pay (the design rule).

### The treasury's plinths

Five chests, one per tier, stand on plinths in the same room. Each closes again after the time in the table, so they cannot be farmed.

## The shops

Say **"Let's trade."** to a keeper. The window shows their shelf and your Pneuka Box. Click to buy or sell.

Prices follow stock, as in Old School RuneScape:

| Rule | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Each unit short of the shelf's stock | 10% dearer |
| Each unit you sell it | 7% cheaper to sell, down to 25% of worth |
| A keeper's non-trade goods are bought at | 50% of worth |
| Stock and glut move one unit back every | 90 real seconds |

**Worth** is what a thing is worth in cubes: the base every price moves from. It comes from the economy table (a lure is 40 cubes, a
brass key 16, a twin key 80, a Lockheart coffin 160 to 240).

| Keeper | Where | Sells | Buys |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raku | the Weir's treasury | Possibilikeys and Lockheart coffins at 145% of worth (haggle) | curios at worth; keys, coffins, maps, crude, materials, fish, lures and the rest at 50% |
| Old Grog | the Weir's pier | lures, casks of crude, Lachrymato Bottles (small 80, medium 200, large 400), the Wake Whistle (64) | fish at fair prices; lures and crude |
| Saggar | the kiln | looks, fired onto you | nothing |
| The Purser | Margarite's dock | casks of mirth and wonder crude | crude, Cogitomaps, rutters, materials |

### Haggling with Raku

Right click a price at Raku's. He asks 145% of worth and never goes below 102%. You can offer one of three prices, flatter him (less each
time), clink cubes on the counter (once; it warms him, but his floor rises), or walk away. A lowball sulks him. Steady fair offers,
flattery or walking away while he is pleased end about 18 to 20% under his list.

### The Purser

The Purser is the trader on Margarite's dock. They post a price and never haggle. They pay by the island's **demand**, which drifts
between 0.75 and 1.35 of worth on a slow clock of 3 to 7 game days. Each unit you sell lowers it by 3% (crude, 0.4%), and it recovers
one unit every 6 real minutes. Margarite pays well for crude and maps, because the King's island wants its minds charted.

## Crude

Crude is liquid Lachryma carried as casks in the Pneuka Box. A ship's hold says how many may cross (a sloop holds 8, a tanker 60). It is
graded by feeling, and the lighter grades are cheaper and safer:

| Grade | Worth a cask | Chance to spill (higher is worse) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mirth | 4 cubes | x0.5 |
| Wonder | 6 | x0.8 |
| Desire | 6 | x1.0 |
| Grief | 8 | x1.2 |
| Dread | 10 | x1.6 |

Only crude spills. If a stage fails with crude aboard, it spills with a chance that rises with the grade and the cargo size. Cubes never
spill. Entropolis sells crude cheap and Margarite buys it dear, so knowing when to sail is the skill. See [The Emocean](emocean.md).

## The kiln

**F** at the kiln's mouth in the workshop. Pick a look for each region of your clay body. **Fire** keeps a look. **Mend** refires the
cracks. Looks sit on the folk's clay ladder: earthenware (free), stoneware (80 cubes), porcelain (200), the Court (480), and the
Prince's own, which is never sold, only earned. Nothing at the kiln changes a number in play.
