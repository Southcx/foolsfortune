# Garden genres: monster raising, monster collection, cozy farming, fungi and trees

Research for the Spirit Garden (Inner Realm). Kept by Dovina. Date: 2026-10-08. Status: research, not a ruling; nothing here is built or
approved, and every idea in section 9 is a proposal for the owner.

**What this is.** A survey of the genres the Spirit Garden descends from (Chao Garden, Monster Rancher, Rune Factory, Harvest Moon,
Legend of Mana, Stardew Valley and their kin), of fungi as "master transmutators" (the owner's wife's request), and of the mythic trees,
distilled into patterns, themes, visuals, pitfalls and ideas for a game about feelings, clay and a hue-wheel soul colour. Words follow
`docs/GLOSSARY.md`: the Courier is "they"; the five feelings are Wonder, Mirth, Desire, Grief, Dread in that order; times name their
clock (game day, game hour, real minute).

**How sourced.** Part of this was looked up on 2026-10-08 (URLs inline, and in the sources list at the end, marked WEB). Part is
design knowledge of the games from memory; those lines are marked MEMORY and should be checked before any number is lifted into
`ECON` or `progress/spirits.js`. Where a search could not confirm something, that is said. A number marked FAN comes from a community
guide, not the developer. Nothing in this file has been tested in our build.

**What the Garden already has** (`docs/plans/SPIRIT-GARDEN.md`, `SOUL-ALCHEMY.md`): six planetoids, beds where a material planted grows
more of its kind, bound spirits (caught Figments) with five stats one a feeling, merging in the cocoon tree, Soul Alchemy's press and
the soul colour, the Firings. Prior art already named there: Black & White, Populous, From Dust, Galaxy, Chao Garden, Monster
Rancher, Jade Cocoon, Spectrobes, Viva Pinata. This file widens the net and then asks what the Garden still lacks.

## 1. The genres in one page

Three families overlap here.

1. **Monster raising** (Chao Garden, Monster Rancher, Digimon World, Jade Cocoon): few creatures, long attachment, the creature's body
   is the save file of what you did. The verb is care: feed, train, rest, watch.
2. **Monster collection and breeding** (Pokemon, Dragon Quest Monsters, Shin Megami Tensei, Ni no Kuni, Slime Rancher, Ooblets): many
   creatures, a combining rule, inheritance. The verb is combine, and the pleasure is the chart you slowly learn.
3. **Cozy farming and crafting** (Harvest Moon, Rune Factory, Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing, Atelier, Spiritfarer): a calendar, a
   plot, a conversion chain. The verb is tend, and the pleasure is the day with nothing wrong in it.

The shared skeleton, found in nearly all of them: **a small place you come to love, on a clock slower than your attention, where
effort turns into a thing you can look at, and where the clock itself (seasons, ageing, the Everdoor) carries feeling.**

## 2. Source by source

For each: what the player DOES (verb loop), what it TEACHES, what makes it COZY or WONDROUS, VISUALS, NUMBERS.

### 2.1 Rune Factory 4 (and Frontier, RF5)

- **Does.** Farm, fight dungeons, tame monsters, craft at forge/pharmacy/cooking stations, court villagers. Monster barns house
  tamed monsters, which then work the farm (water, plant, harvest, depending on kind) and give produce (wool, eggs, milk).
  WEB: taming requires a barn with space; two barns at the start, four monsters each; you throw an item at a monster, a heart bubble is
  success, a skull failure; the Monster Brush raises the odds when used first
  (https://www.thegamer.com/rune-factory-4-special-monster-farming-taming/, https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=634794). The wiki says
  early targets are Woolys and Cluckadoodles and "pretty much anything" tames them: the first taming is a gift to the player, later
  ones want the right item (gift preference).
- **Rune points and rune spheres.** WEB (weak): RP is the stamina-like resource for tools, magic and attacks; crops yield "rune
  spheres" when ripe in other entries (RF2's glowing blue sphere over a ripe plot restores RP,
  https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-23516/). MEMORY: the idea in RF1 to RF4 is that every farm action costs RP, and harvesting or
  the right food gives it back: the farm literally powers the adventurer. It is a closed loop between the two halves of the game.
- **Crop levels and giant crops.** MEMORY (not confirmed by search, which only confirmed that giant crops and trees were new in RF4,
  https://boardgamegeek.com/videogame/124519/rune-factory-4): each crop has a level raised by watering and fertilising and by
  "growth" of the field; a crop at high level sells for more and can make a *large* version; planting a 2x2 block of one crop at the
  right level can fuse into a *giant* crop that yields many items for one harvest. The lesson: **adjacency and care make a bigger
  single thing**, a shape you can see.
- **Seasons.** Each crop belongs to a season; out of season it will not grow (outside the greenhouse-like Rune Factory plot
  tricks). Festivals mark the turn.
- **Forge loop.** Ore, wood, scrap go in; a recipe and a skill level decide the result; items have stats, gem slots, and can be
  upgraded by "synthesis". The craft is a levelling skill: doing it raises the ability to do it better (a design law of ours: skill
  skips grind, grind closes the gap).
- **Rune Factory Frontier (Wii).** MEMORY: Runeys are small spirits that live on the farm; each plot and surrounding area has a Runey
  count, and the player keeps the ecosystem healthy by harvesting and by not over-using the land; Runeys feed on and are fed by the
  land and make crops and tools more effective. It is an early "ecosystem balance as a stat". Treat details as unverified.
- **RF5 (briefly).** MEMORY: adds Rigbarths (rideable monster mounts), monster friendship levels as in-battle help, and the
  Sechs Empire seasons; mostly a refinement of RF4. Not researched further.
- **Teaches.** That tending and fighting feed each other; that a creature befriended is a worker, not just a pet.
- **Cozy/wondrous.** The day has a rhythm of morning chores, noon adventure, evening cooking; a monster that follows you is company.
- **Visuals.** Anime bust-up portraits, bright pastel fields, chibi sprites, a day-bar clock top-left, a stamina gauge.

### 2.2 Harvest Moon: A Wonderful Life (GameCube/PS2, 2003)

- **Does.** One farm over a lifetime: the game is divided into **chapters of years** (MEMORY: chapters 1 to 3 as young adult, then
  later chapters with your child grown, ending with them taking over). You marry, a child is born, the child grows through toddler and
  school age to a young adult who decides what to do with the farm. Cows and chickens are the main animals; **the ranch is life**.
- **Hybrids and Daryl.** WEB: a forum says Daryl gives a free seed maker if you befriend him; Destructoid advises giving Daryl
  fossils and old coins to raise friendship until he gives you things (https://www.destructoid.com/a-wonderful-life-tips-story-of-seasons/).
  Another source ties seed combination to Vinnie, from Chapter 2 (https://archive.rpgamer.com/games/harvest/hmawl/hmawlprev.html and a
  supercheats thread); the search could not confirm which character unlocks it, so check. MEMORY: a seed maker lets two crops'
  seeds combine into hybrids (melon-like cross crops), so **a stranger's friendship unlocks a new kind of making**.
- **Dig site.** WEB: Carter directs the dig, which starts small and grows over time (same preview). Fossils and old coins feed
  friendship and the museum-like collection.
- **Teaches.** Time. The game's real subject is that the same place is different when the child is ten, and when you are old.
- **Cozy/wondrous.** Melancholy, soft: the seasons turn and people age; the Harvest Goddess and sprites live at the edge.
- **Visuals.** Soft low-poly 3D, warm light, long dusk, a diary or calendar. The seasonal palette shifts the whole screen.

### 2.3 Legend of Mana (PS1, 1999) and its orchard

- **Does.** Place "land artefacts" on a world map to create locations; at the home you tend an orchard, a pet corral, a golem
  workshop, instrument and produce crafting. WEB: once the Mana Orchard mission is done you grow fruit southwest of the house by
  bringing seeds to **Trent**, the ancient tree; seeds come from enemy drops or from Trent himself; eight kinds from common Small
  Seeds to the rare Spiny Seed; at most two seeds can be entrusted to Trent at a time; picking unripe fruit loses it and Trent
  warns you; output depends on the seeds' number and kind and on the home area's mana levels
  (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Legend_of_Mana/Produce_and_Seeds, https://lparchive.org/Legend-of-Mana-(by-Mega64)/Update 05).
  A let's-play write-up says growth also depends on "the day of the week" (the elemental days: Moon, Fire, Water, Wood, Gold,
  Earth, Sun); the search could not give the exact table. MEMORY: the game's world has Wisp, Shade, Dryad, Salamander, Undine,
  Gnome, Jinn, Lumina elementals; day and place weights decide which elemental flavour of produce grows.
- **Lil' Cactus.** MEMORY: Lil' Cactus keeps a diary (his entries are visible) and leaves you "Cactus" gifts; **a small character's
  record of you, in their own voice, is a reward.**
- **Pets and eggs.** MEMORY: monster eggs found or bought hatch into pets, whose growth depends on food and a hidden stat set; they
  can be raised to fight. **Golems** are built from parts and elemental content; **instruments** are crafted and played.
- **Teaches.** That a place has moods (mana, element, weekday) and that you plant with those in mind, not against them.
- **Cozy/wondrous.** The talking tree as a person; hand-painted pastel storybook art; strange, quiet music; no explanation.
- **Visuals.** Painted storybook backgrounds, pastel fantasy, thick outlines on sprites, elemental colour per spirit.

### 2.4 Monster Rancher

- **Does.** Raise one monster from a disc or data; train it with drills; feed it; rest it; send it to tournaments; it ages and dies,
  leaving a *memory* that can be combined with another. Monsters are **generated from the data of any CD/disc** (the game reads
  the disc's data): real-world objects become creatures; MEMORY: the type is a function of the disc's hash, which is why a
  favourite album yields a favourite monster. Our Veritome plate is already this.
- **Care numbers.** WEB (Monster Rancher DS/4 guides, FAN): stress and fatigue drain lifespan; bands of 0 to 299 cost nothing, 900 to
  999 cost 22 lifespan a week, and a bad week can lose 52 with both over 900; drills run 4 weeks but apply 2 weeks of lifespan decay;
  hunger at "Hungry" costs lifespan; Meat adds 1 week each time eaten; Taffy restores fatigue; Mint Leaf restores stress; food
  likes and dislikes give a bonus or penalty to bonding and stress relief, and food choice shapes body type
  (https://legendcup.com/faq-mrds-lifespan-info.php, https://legendcup.com/MRM/discus/messages/12/16540b0c.php,
  https://almarsguides.com/retro/walkthroughs/ps1/games/monsterrancher/misc/itemuses/,
  https://legendcup.com/faq-mr4ranchingbasics.php). Leniency in MR4 makes monsters live longer and stress less but obey less.
  The disc-generation question was not answered by search; above is MEMORY.
- **Teaches.** Optimisation: the community invented rest-avoiding schedules (the "Energizer Bunny" method) because the numbers are
  legible.
- **Cozy/wondrous.** The creature's growing from a baby to a hulk with your fingerprints on it; the sadness of the end.
- **Visuals.** Menu-driven, calendar-in-weeks, a status panel with stat bars, a mascot trainer.

### 2.5 Dragon Quest Monsters, Pokemon, Shin Megami Tensei, Ni no Kuni

MEMORY unless noted; none searched.

- **Dragon Quest Monsters (Joker, Terry's Wonderland).** Synthesis/breeding: two parents (male and female, or any two) produce a
  child of a family, often by *specific parent families*; child starts at level 1 but inherits skill points and chosen skills; "plus
  value" accumulates across generations; family trees show lineage. Lesson: **the parents' effort is poured into the child's
  starting line**; the grind is paid forward.
- **Pokemon.** Daycare: two compatible monsters left together produce an egg (hatched by walking steps); egg moves, natures
  (+10 percent / -10 percent to two stats), IVs (genetic 0 to 31 per stat), EVs (effort values 510 total, 252 per stat, earned by
  defeating specific species). Lesson: **a hidden genetic layer and a visible effort layer**, which is a precedent for our
  "grain" (temperament) and "stats".
- **Shin Megami Tensei.** Demon fusion: two demons merge into a third by **race** pair (a chart: Fairy + Beast = Divine, etc.), the
  result's level around the parents' average; Cathedral of Shadows; sacrificial/accident fusions. Lesson: the **fusion chart is the
  game**, a lookup learned by experiment.
- **Ni no Kuni.** Familiars with "metamorphosis" at set levels, tamed by catching with a special crystal, fed treats that raise
  a stat and *tuned to a flavour* (Ghibli look, Studio Ghibli character design). Lesson: **a creature's evolution is a cutscene
  with feeling**.

### 2.6 Slime Rancher

- **Does.** First-person rancher on a far planet. Vacuum slimes, feed them diets (Pink slimes eat fruit and veg, etc.), collect the
  **plorts** (their droppings, a crystallised product) and sell them. WEB: a **largo** is a slime that ate another kind's plort and
  takes on both traits and diets, producing two plorts instead of one; if a largo eats a third plort it becomes a **tarr**, which
  rots nearby produce and multiplies; the fix is water (https://techraptor.net/content/slime-rancher-beginner-guide,
  https://gamespot.com/articles/slime-rancher-2-tips-for-new-players-avoiding-the-tarr-plort-pricing-and-more/1100-6508028/).
  Market: oversupply drops a plort's price from its base (Pink 7 Newbucks), recovery takes several in-game days, arrows on the
  board show direction, and new slimes found tend to depress prices of the old; players sell in batches
  (steam discussions linked in the same results; FAN).
- **Teaches.** A market is a living thing: selling hurts the price.
- **Cozy/wondrous.** Squishy cute things with faces; plorts as little jewels; Tarrs as pure comic horror.
- **Visuals.** Saturated pastel jelly, translucent shaders, glowing gems, a bright toy sky.

### 2.7 Ooblets, Spiritfarer, Digimon World, Chao Garden

- **Ooblets** (MEMORY): dance battles to win seeds; plant seeds that grow Ooblets in the field; they are collected as crops;
  friendship; a town of weird folk. The creature *is* the crop. Pastel, rubber-hose bounce.
- **Spiritfarer.** WEB: Stella, ferrymaster, sails a ship to gather spirits, fulfils their wishes, builds kitchens and gardens and

  spirit flower in memory; the spirits are often people from Stella's own life; the game is a life sim about death

- **Digimon World** (PS1). WEB (weak, many low-quality copies): care mistakes (neglect, wrong training, overfeeding) shape which
  evolution branch results, fewer mistakes unlock better; overfeeding raises weight; in other entries (DMX) some stages require a

- **Chao Garden** (Sonic Adventure 1 and 2, the owner's first inspiration). WEB: stats are graded E to S and the grade sets how

  and Power; alignment (neutral, Hero, Dark) set by how the Chao is treated in its first stage; first evolution resets stat

  least two reincarnations; Devil/Angel by Dark/Hero fruit after two reincarnations; death shows ascent or descent by alignment
  (https://info.sonicretro.org/Chao, https://retroachievements.org/forums/topic/16197,
  https://www.sonicstadium.org/timewarp/v7/games-v7/g_sa2/chaohint/ ; the ria-compute and cyberpost pages were judged low quality
  and not used). MEMORY: animal parts (ears, wings, feet, tail) are borrowed from the small animals the Chao was given, a visible
  inheritance of diet; **reincarnation** keeps a little of the previous life.

## 3. Fungi as transmutators

The owner's wife asked for mushrooms and fungal entities as "master transmutators": a curio put in a bed that breaks down or
transforms into new curios. The real biology is richer than the trope. Citations WEB unless marked.

### 3.1 The real behaviours

1. **Decomposition.** Fungi are the planet's great recyclers: they release enzymes outside the body, dissolve dead matter and take
   it in (MEMORY, standard mycology). The lesson is that **breaking down is the first half of making**: the dead log feeds the forest.
2. **Mycoremediation.** Fungi detoxify soil, break down petroleum-based toxins and capture ash and heavy metals; a Northern
   California project inoculates straw wattles with a native fungus to filter wildfire ash below burned homes
   (https://www.fox13now.com/news/national/scientists-harness-mushrooms-to-detoxify-soil-and-protect-waterways-after-wildfires,
   https://www.yankodesign.com/2022/12/04/prototype-can-release-mushroom-spores-to-remove-toxicity-in-wildfire-hit-areas/amp/).
   **The cleanse**: a fungus that takes a spoiled thing (a grief-soaked curio) and returns it neutral.
3. **Mycorrhizal networks, the "wood wide web".** Suzanne Simard's work popularised trees sharing carbon and warnings through
   fungal threads; a Nature Ecology & Evolution citation review argued the evidence is thinner and the claims more widespread than the
   data (citation bias; hard to tell which filaments connect which trees); Simard defends her work
   (https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6758773, https://inverse.com/science/wood-wide-web-debunk-study,
   https://www.nzgeo.com/?p=481501). **Design note:** the myth is more useful than the dispute; the game can say it as folk belief
   ("the folk say the roots talk") and keep the claim modest.
4. **Fairy rings.** MEMORY: mushrooms fruiting in a ring where the mycelium has grown outward from a centre, the centre dying
   as the ring expands; folklore says doorways to the fae. **A ring is a growth front and a portal.**
5. **Bioluminescence and foxfire.** MEMORY (search did not cover): a few fungi (Armillaria, Panellus, Mycena) glow green in
   rotting wood; "foxfire" or "kitsune-bi" in Japan; a constant cold glow. Our night-glowing Lachryma is the natural cousin.
6. **Ophiocordyceps (zombie fungi).** MEMORY: infects ants, steers behaviour, fruits from the head; The Last of Us made it a
   pandemic. **A fungus that rewrites a mind** is the dark mirror of the Veritome's reprogramming; we should use it only as a
   hazard (Dread), not the Garden's friend.
7. **Slime moulds (Physarum).** WEB: Nakagaki (2000) showed Physarum finding the shortest path through a maze by spreading,
   connecting food sources and retracting inefficient tubes; in 2010 it built networks comparable to the Tokyo rail system;
   molecular work reclassified slime moulds as Amoebozoa, not fungi
   (https://aeon.co/essays/what-can-slime-mould-teach-us-about-biological-memory, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.02910,
   https://www.mcgill.ca/bioengineering-hyperbook/article/physics-superorganisms-2024/slime-mold). **A pathfinder which has no
   brain**: perfect for a god game where water, veins and roads are puzzles. Honest labelling: a slime mould is not a mushroom, so
   call it a "slime" in the Garden and let the folk say what it is.
8. **Lichens.** MEMORY: a fungus and an alga or cyanobacterium in a partnership so total it is named as one thing; colonises bare
   rock, makes the first soil. **Symbiosis as a verb: two things that became a third.** Direct model for a "pair in a bed" rule.
9. **Koji and fermentation.** MEMORY: *Aspergillus oryzae* turns rice into sake, soy into miso and shoyu, with time and warmth;
   a kura (brewery) has a ritual and a guardian spirit; the farm's cellar in Stardew is the same instinct. **Transformation that
   improves a thing without hiding what it was.**
10. **Penicillium.** MEMORY: Fleming's 1928 contaminated plate where mould cleared the bacteria around it; a lucky accident
    noticed by a prepared mind. It is the founding story for "the mistake that proves a cure", which suits our Luck system
    (surprise lived through). A kintsugi-adjacent idea: **a flaw turned into the feature.**
11. **Kintsugi parallel.** Our own world already has it (`kintsugi`, the gold that shows while a crack mends). A fungus
    that mends a crack with a visible seam is canon-adjacent: "mycelium kintsugi" is a real design practice (MEMORY).

### 3.3 The shape of a fungal transmutator (distilled)

A bed with a fungus is a **three-state conversion**: *food* (a curio), *time*, *product*. What is distinct from a Stardew keg:

- It **decomposes before it builds**: the first state is rot or break-down (visible), the second is the new thing (visible).
- It is **species-specific**: one fungus likes wood, another bone, another cloth (substrates), and each knows its own
  transformations (decomposition's chemistry).
- It is **networked**: neighbouring beds share a mycelium, so the fungus is also a *carrier of feeling* between plots.
- It is **grown from a spore** that you carry and a **spawn run** in a substrate: the verbs are inoculate, wait, fruit, harvest.
- It leaves a **residue** (compost, a ring), so the bed gets better as it is used.

## 5. Distillation 1: recurring mechanics (numbered patterns)

1. **The growth stage ladder.** A thing passes seed, sprout, ripe (stage names shown as pictures, not numbers). Stardew crops,
   Slime Rancher produce, Chao Garden egg/child/adult/evolution, Monster Rancher birth to death. Our blot (stage a game day) fits.
2. **Time as an ingredient.** Casks (2 seasons), peach cycles (3,000 / 6,000 / 9,000 years), Legend of Mana's weekday, koji, Rune
   Factory seasons. The wait is where the game is cozy.
3. **Quality as a visible step** (silver/gold/iridium stars; Atelier quality; Chao grades E to S). A small pictorial ladder multiplies
   value by a known factor and invites optimisation.
4. **Adjacency makes a bigger thing** (Rune Factory and Stardew giant crops; Animal Crossing's checkerboard; Slime Rancher corral
   neighbours). Layout is a verb without a button.
5. **Two combine, a third emerges** (DQM breeding, SMT fusion by race, Slime Rancher largos, Harvest Moon hybrids, Animal Crossing
   genes, Atelier trait transfer, lichen). The chart is learned, not told.
6. **Inheritance of effort** (DQM plus values, Pokemon EVs, Atelier traits, Chao reincarnation keeping a trace). The grind is paid
   forward; this fits our "grind closes the gap".
7. **Hidden genes behind a visible phenotype** (Pokemon IVs/natures, Animal Crossing hybrid red, Daryl-style seed makers). The
   reveal is a pleasure.
8. **Likes and dislikes** (Monster Rancher food, RF gifts, Spiritfarer meals, Stardew gifts). Learning a creature is learning its
   taste; wrong gifts teach as much as right ones.
9. **Care meters with a soft floor** (Monster Rancher stress and fatigue, Digimon care mistakes). Good designs make neglect a branch
   (Digimon's alternate forms) rather than a punishment; bad ones kill the pet.
10. **Stat = what was done** (Chao: swim animals raise swim; RF: skill by use). The body is a ledger; our Veritome idea.
11. **Closed loop between two halves** (Rune Factory: farm powers adventure and adventure feeds farm; Slime Rancher: plorts pay for
    expansions that make more plorts). The Garden's equivalent: Wells feed the Garden, the Garden's spirits feed the Wells.
12. **A market with a memory** (Slime Rancher's plort price drop and recovery; Animal Crossing's turnip prices). Selling is a
    decision and a mistake can be waited out.
13. **A collection that opens the world** (Stardew bundles, museums, bestiaries, Chao's races). The completion is a door, not a
    checklist.
14. **A permanent either-or** (Stardew's mushroom cave vs bats; Joja vs Center). Gives a run an identity; criticised when one
    choice is obviously better. WEB: the guides say mushrooms win for bundles.
15. **An NPC that grows with you and is eventually let go** (Spiritfarer's Everdoor, Harvest Moon's child taking over,
    Monster Rancher's death and memory, Chao reincarnation). The care loop has an end that is a gift.
16. **A tree or shrine as the hub** (Trent, Kalpavriksha, Yggdrasil, Mulberry Grove). A talking centre gives the place a mouth.
17. **The environment as a hidden multiplier** (Legend of Mana's mana level and weekday; Rune Factory's Runeys; Frontier's balance;
    Stardew's season). The place has moods, and the best player reads them.
18. **Helpers** (Rune Factory monsters that water and harvest; Stardew's Junimos; Animal Crossing visitors watering). The farm
    starts to work without you, which is the fantasy of a mature garden. Animal Crossing's 5 percent versus 80 percent figure
    (WEB) is a good model: **guests multiply what you do**.
19. **Failure as comedy** (Slime Rancher's tarr, Viva Pinata's ruffians, Harvest Moon's weeds): a mistake is a visible, funny,
    remediable mess with a clear fix (water on the tarr).
20. **Craft as a map** (Potion Craft, Atelier trait loop, our Soul Alchemy): choose ingredients as vectors; the picture is the
    answer.

## 6. Distillation 2: recurring themes and emotions

- **Slowness as care.** The cozy promise is that nothing is lost by pausing. Slow clocks (Stardew's ten real minutes a game day;
  our game day is a real hour) are part of the genre's comfort.
- **Mortality and succession.** A Wonderful Life's child, Monster Rancher's death, Spiritfarer's Everdoor, Cult of the Lamb's
  followers. The genre quietly teaches that things end and that the ending can be tender.
- **Becoming known to a place.** Villagers learn your name; your farm learns your habits; Lil' Cactus writes about you.
- **Reciprocity.** The tree gives fruit, you give seeds; the spirit gives its wishes, you give a room; visitors water the flowers.
- **Strangeness accepted.** Odd gifts, odd folk, odd rules; the world is not explained. Legend of Mana is the high mark.
- **Cleansing and renewal.** Mycoremediation, the Tarr washed away, the new season, the Heavenly Kiln.
- **Hidden networks.** The wood wide web, spirit veins, Physarum: a quiet connection under the visible field.
- **Gentle awe.** The giant crop, the first iridium star, the first evolution cutscene. Wonder arrives as scale or as glow.
- **Grief as a form of love** (Spiritfarer). Our Grief feeling needs a place that is not a punishment.
- **Mischief and the cute-cruel** (Cult of the Lamb, Slime Rancher's Tarr). Dread can be funny.

## 7. Distillation 3: recurring visuals

- **Palette.** Pastel and warm: sunlit greens, butter yellows, sky blue, peach; a dark cave for fungus with luminous accent (the
  Mushroom Cave's purples and the foxfire's green). Seasons recolour the whole screen. Night is blue, with lights warm.
- **Motion.** Gentle bobbing idle loops; squash-and-stretch; creatures hop; plants sway; pollen and fireflies drift; water ripples.
  Growth shown by scale and a small pop of sparkle; evolution by a bright flash and silhouette.
- **UI.** A clock and a calendar as a corner dial; a stamina or RP gauge; a bust-up portrait on dialogue; an inventory grid of
  small icons; a quality star or ribbon on an item; a heart bubble above a creature (RF's heart vs skull taming bubble is a
  perfect world mark: it carries no words). A diary page as a reward (Lil' Cactus).
- **Forms.** Round, soft, big-eyed; spirits with animal heads (Spiritfarer); translucent jelly (Slime Rancher); crystals as
  products (plorts); trees with faces (Trent).
- **Materials.** Wood, wool, glass, clay, paper: tactile and hand-made. Hand-painted textures. The ceramics of our own world already
  fit.
- **Light.** Dusk-gold, dappled under trees, lantern light in dark caves; glow as a status.
- **Fungal language.** Rings, caps in colour families, gills as pattern, thin white threads (mycelium) running under soil, spore
  motes drifting up as dust; bioluminescent green-blue; a *cut-away view of the soil* (the ant-farm look).

## 8. Distillation 4: pitfalls and criticisms

All genre-wide, mostly MEMORY and widely repeated by players and reviewers; the search results for Rune Factory and Slime Rancher
support the market-recovery and farm-helper points (WEB, as above).

1. **Grind and chores.** Daily watering and feeding of many plots/animals; the same ten actions every game day. Fixes in the
   genre: sprinklers, helper monsters (Rune Factory), auto-feeders. Our law: skill skips grind.
2. **Inventory bloat.** Hundreds of near-identical items; storage chests as a second game (Stardew). Atelier's category slots help.
3. **Punishing decay.** Pets that die of neglect (Monster Rancher stress/fatigue 22 to 52 lifespan a week at worst, WEB FAN;
   Digimon World), crops that wilt, relationships that decay. Cozy design avoids permanent loss; Spiritfarer and our "bound until
   released" do.
4. **Opaque systems.** Hidden stats and unclear recipes force wiki use (Chao Garden numbers disputed even among fans, WEB; Legend
   of Mana's day-of-week table not shown in the game). The charm of mystery fights fairness; our Veritome/Crib Sheet is an answer.
5. **The best strategy is a spreadsheet.** Monster Rancher's Energizer Bunny training methods, Stardew's optimised layouts. Players
   optimise the fun away; a game must keep "good enough" pleasant.
6. **Dominant choices.** The Stardew mushroom cave vs bats (WEB: mushrooms are the stronger).
7. **Market exploitation and collapse.** Slime Rancher prices dropping when saturated and when a new slime appears (WEB, anecdotal);
   cozy players dislike having to pick a "best" plort.
8. **Time-gating that fights real life.** Real-time crops, daily login. Our game day is a real hour (kept in `calendar.js`).
9. **Content far from the loop.** Dungeon runs required to unlock farming (Rune Factory) or farming required to pay for dungeons.
10. **Romance and marriage as a checklist.** Not our concern, but the genre is criticised for it.
11. **Death used cheaply** (Digimon World's evolve-time kill if over 5 care mistakes in DMX, WEB; Cult followers dying).
12. **Hybrid breeding's trial and error** (Animal Crossing hybrid flower trees are mostly learned from wikis, WEB).

## 9. Ten outside-the-box ideas for Fool's Fortune

Each says what it is, what it borrows, how it fits the glossary and what could go wrong. All are proposals; none is ruled.

1. **The Fungus Bed (a bed that digests curios).** A new kind of bed with a spore and a substrate. Place a curio in; it goes
   through *rot* (visible, a few game hours), then *flush* (the mushroom fruiting), then yields one or more new items by a
   **recipe of kinds** (like Atelier categories): a spoiled cask of dread crude becomes a grey-blue lamp-cap that, eaten or pressed,
   gives a Dread-hued material. Borrow: Stardew keg and cask, Atelier categories, mycology. Fit: beds already grow materials;
   this adds *transformation*. Risk: an inventory sink; limit to three conversions at a time.
2. **The Spore Wheel.** Fungi are *not* the five feelings; they are the **five phases' decomposers**. Assign each feeling a
   fungus family (moss-like Wonder, ember-like Mirth, truffle-like Desire, bracket-like Grief, ink-cap Dread) so the Spirit Garden's
   own wheel appears in the mushrooms' colours. A fungus **moves a material's hue one step round the wheel** (a transmutation in the
   Soul Alchemy sense) instead of changing it entirely. Borrow: Soul Alchemy's soul colour, Potion Craft's map. Risk: the colour
   puzzle becomes a duplicate of the press; solve by making the fungus *slow, the press fast*.
3. **Mycelium as the garden's second network.** Under the soil, white threads (a drawn overlay when the hand digs) join beds.
   A fungus in one bed shares a *fraction* of its feeling with its neighbours' beds; the god hand can cut or lead threads, as it
   leads water. Borrow: wood wide web (WEB, with its dispute noted), Physarum's maze-solving (WEB). Fit: the hand's verbs and the
   garden's water. Risk: the dispute; present it as the folk's belief.
4. **Physarum as the planetoid's road-maker.** A "slime" (named so; not a fungus) that you seed at two beds: over a game hour it grows
   the shortest path and the hand can feed it to retract the loops. The result is a path that spirits prefer. Borrow: Nakagaki's maze
   (WEB), Tokyo rail experiment (WEB). Fit: a spirit's errand route. Risk: pathfinding cost; do it as a precomputed graph.
5. **The Arbor Dianae: a silver tree that grows from displacement.** In the Athanor, a glass vessel: drop a copper curio (the
   branch) into a Grief-hued solution and a **silver tree** grows over real minutes; its fruit are tiny silver beads that count as a
   rare material. The alchemists' own proof that minerals live (WEB). Borrow: the classic demonstration (WEB). Fit: Soul
   Alchemy's Athanor. Risk: a one-off toy; give it a rare yield and a calendar (one a game day).
6. **A wishing tree with ten faces (Kalpavriksha).** The Mulberry Grove's tree grants *categories* of gift the way the Jain tree
   grants garments, utensils, food, music, ornaments, light (WEB): ten categories, each unlocked by a bound spirit's wish
   fulfilled (Spiritfarer). Wishing is paid in Lachryma of the matching feeling. Fit: the Garden's dividend. Risk: too like a shop;
   keep every wish a *picture* with no price.
7. **Peach tiers (the Banquet).** A rare tree in the Pavilions of Echoes, three tiers ripening on 1, 3 and 9 game days (scaled down
   from 3,000, 6,000 and 9,000 years, WEB). On the ninth, a **banquet** invites every bound spirit to a feast; the Courier's only
   action is to set the table. Spirits gain a notch of bond for attending. Borrow: Xiwangmu's banquet (WEB). Risk: calendar
   anxiety; make a missed banquet delay, never cancel.
8. **Weather as a bed's mood (Legend of Mana's weekday).** A bed's yield is bent by the **feeling of the day's weather** (we have
   `game.weather`); a Mirth day favours fire-phase fungi. No table is shown: the bed's glow hints at it (cozy because you can read
   it by eye). Borrow: Legend of Mana (WEB), Stardew seasons. Fit: the five phases already in SPIRIT-GARDEN.md.
9. **Letting go as a mechanic (the Everdoor, in clay).** A bound spirit can be *released through a kiln*: fired into a **pot**
   that holds its last gift (a glaze, a seed, a line of its song) and stands in the Garden forever. The Courier feels the cost of
   a release. Borrow: Spiritfarer (WEB), Chao reincarnation (WEB), Monster Rancher memories. Fit: clay and ceramics; the Heavenly
   Kiln. Risk: SPIRIT-GARDEN.md says "bound until released" and "a spirit never fades or dies"; this idea honours that by making release
   the player's choice, a gift not a loss.
10. **The mistake as a form (Penicillium).** A *contaminated* press or bed gives a **spoiled** result that, with luck, becomes a
    new unique material (Fleming, MEMORY). The Luck system (surprise in bits) reads it. Digimon's care mistakes (WEB) as a branch:
    neglect shapes a different, equally valid form. Borrow: Penicillium, Digimon. Risk: reward neglect; the cure is that the
    surprise only pays once per kind.

Bonus (not counted): **a fairy ring is a portal**. A ring of mushrooms that, after a game day, links two planetoids' beds so
spirits cross without launching (MEMORY, folklore). It uses the Launch lotus's slot differently.
