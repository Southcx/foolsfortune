# Research: the rail shooters' set pieces, and what they teach (2026-10-08)

Gathered for the crossing's overhaul (`docs/plans/RAIL-OVERHAUL.md`) by a research agent for Dovina. Primary sources were scarce (no
GDC transcripts or postmortems reachable); fan wikis gave numbers without design commentary; "inference" marks the researcher's own
reading. **Video links are matched by title only, timestamps unverified**: watch before trusting them.

## Kingdom Hearts II: the Gummi Ship missions

Each route (Asteroid Sweep, Stardust Sweep, Phantom Storm, Sunlight Storm, Ancient Highway, Broken Highway, Splash Island, Floating
Island, Assault of the Dreadnought) is played three ways, ranked S to E: **medals** (S 30), **kills** (S typically 500), **score**
(S 1,000,000, then S+1 .. S+10), each with an **EX challenge** (a build rule: 50 HP or fewer, projectile Gummies only). (khwiki:
https://www.khwiki.com/Phantom_Storm and siblings.)
- **Phantom Storm** is the best one: a green tunnel of buildings, then **the Pirate Ship**, one giant enemy with parts that pay
  separately (the wheel and shaft jump two medal ranks; cannons that knock you back; a metal shield over the skull that must open;
  Grapplers that latch on and are shaken off with a roll; the skull releases ghouls). Mission 2 is scripted: behind, a shorter return
  pass at the same place, **the camera to the front** with debris, the ship again, a Driller (then red, then gold, each worth more),
  the ship a last time. Mission 3: an ambush from behind, then from the front, then waves from every side.
- **Asteroid Sweep:** a red Spider boss twice (a laser barrage you roll; appendages before the torso); a red alert beep and direction
  for threats off screen; gold and red variants spawn when a group is cleared.
- **Splash Island:** clearing every Bomber group early summons a gold Bomber; Shield groups a gold Shield.
- Fans: "very Star Fox 64-ish"; "fine but forgettable"; the standing complaint is that it cannot be sped up or skipped.
- Videos: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rwVFnkC3_OE (Phantom Storm, all three), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q9wLPMpT7uU,
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4RaO2WoMU4 (every mission 3), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3xIG9lcb5ww (Dreadnought).

## Elemental Gearbolt (PS1, Alfa System, 1997)

First-person rail; aim and shoot only; three spells switched freely, no ammo (fire: one slow strong shot; electricity: a spread;
water: rapid and weak). **No lock or charge mechanic** is described anywhere (the brief's premise may be Panzer Dragoon's or Rez's).
Attacks are telegraphed by **shrinking boxes round the enemy**; a consecutive-hit combo builds score and experience and one miss breaks
it; the stage score is split between points and experience at the end (a score-versus-power trade). "Deliberate film-like pacing":
stages either tightly packed and chaotic or wide open to show emptiness. Bosses fill the screen with body and bullets.
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemental_Gearbolt, https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/elemental-gearbolt/,
https://infinityretro.com/elemental-gearbolt-review/.) Videos: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YN8HPX5O6cI (full run).

## Panzer Dragoon (Zwei, Orta, Saga)

- **Zwei:** seven levels, a boss each after the first; a radar for threats from all sides; one reticle for gun and lock-on lasers;
  branching routes in levels 2 to 4; the dragon evolves by score; **Berserk** (filled by shooting) homes lasers on everything on screen;
  ADEC, an adaptive difficulty (the first was "too hard"; some found Zwei too easy). (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzer_Dragoon_Zwei)
- **Orta:** ten episodes, a boss each; 360 degrees, turned in 90-degree steps; speed up and slow down; bosses circled (positioning
  reaches weak points). **Three forms switched at will, each a trade**: Base (balanced, up to 8 locks), Heavy (strongest shot, 3 locks,
  no glide), Glide (mobile, fragile, rapid homing) (sources disagree on the exact numbers); each its own Berserk (brief invulnerability);
  clearing whole waves earns Gene Base (upgrades). Episode 2's boss: a giant manta ray with plants on its back; episode 8: a battleship
  whose arms come off before its back; an airship dozens of miles long. Revived before every boss. Criticised as "multi-target lock then
  swarm of bursts". (https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/panzer-dragoon-orta/) Videos: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-i2BlUOtDo
  (episode 8 boss), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W2fUAUb3Ksc (longplay).
- **Saga:** **a boss is a space**: quadrants round the enemy, a radar marking safe, neutral and dangerous zones; some weak points only
  from danger zones; patterns change mid-fight; moving pauses your gauges. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzer_Dragoon_Saga)

## The others

- **Star Fox 64:** corridor stages and all-range boss arenas; routes branch by objectives; medals need wingmen intact plus a hit total
  (a medal you can fail on a clear); Slippy scans boss shields. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Fox_64)
- **Rez / Rez Infinite:** lock 8, release; every lock a hi-hat, every kill a bit of the track; each area starts with one layer and grows
  to ten by the boss; quantised so anyone plays in time; damage devolves your form (the body is the health bar); Area 5 a boss rush;
  **Area X: free-roaming, no rail** (the non-linear precedent). (https://timeextension.com/features/the-making-of-rez-tetsuya-mizuguchis-timeless-masterpiece)
- **Child of Eden:** lock 8 plus a rapid-fire some enemies need (two verbs for two kinds of target); a full lock released on the beat
  scores; the screen-clear Euphoria costs score and rank (a panic button with a price). (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_of_Eden)
- **Sin and Punishment: Star Successor:** a rail that runs forward, back, sideways, up and down; a sword deflects projectiles; knocked
  back missiles raise the multiplier; bosses made to fit the screen yet look huge; a year's fight over difficulty.
- **Ikaruga:** five chapters, about 25 minutes; same colour absorbed (charges up to twelve homing lasers), opposite kills; chains of
  three of one colour, 100 doubling to 25,600; **every boss second left is worth 10,000**; Buppousou's weak points behind covers that
  open only for the matching polarity; Uzura's ring of 14 turrets spins faster as turrets fall; a limited-ammo prototype was dropped for
  "too many breaks in the action". (https://shmups.wiki/library/Ikaruga)
- **Radiant Silvergun:** six shots from three buttons; bullets slow for a broad audience; tested by high-score players; segmented bosses
  pay a bonus for clearing every appendage before the weak spot.
- **Einhander:** a manipulator arm takes gunpods off downed foes; a mid-boss and a boss a stage, each a core with parts; techno score.
- **The Itano circus** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itano_Circus): missiles of three behaviours at once (straight, predicting,
  zigzag for visibility); lens from telephoto far to fisheye near; aerial-film camera; detonation inward, then the shockwave's collapse.
- **Ace Combat:** superweapon set pieces built as preconditions, then a run into the interior (Megalith, Fortress Intolerance: a silo
  dive to the missile; Excalibur ringed by laser towers; Zero's last duel hits only the intakes, head on).

## Cross-game patterns

1. Never idle: something to lock on or dodge between big moments. 2. A view change is the pacing tool (where threats come from, not
how many). 3. A boss is a space, not a bar. 4. A bonus for clearing a whole group. 5. The panic button has a price. 6. Short and
replayable (Ikaruga 25 minutes, Orta 1 to 2 hours). 7. The body is the health display (Rez). 8. Difficulty was always a fight: keep it
tunable late.

## Lessons to codify (sources above; "inference" is the researcher's)

1. One dominant big object a pass, whose parts pay separately (Phantom Storm). 2. A boss's weak point is a position (Saga, Orta).
3. Safe and danger zones shown without words (Saga's radar). 4. Clear every foe of a group: a bonus foe (Gummi gold variants, Orta).
5. A form change is a trade, not an upgrade (Orta). 6. Forms differ in lock count or range (Orta 8 vs 3; inference for ours).
7. Forms switch at will. 8. A panic button filled by hitting, with a price (Berserk, Euphoria). 9. Lock on up to 8, released together.
10. Release on the beat to score (Child of Eden). 11. Quantise to the beat (Rez). 12. Each lock and kill a layer of music, the boss the
thickest (Rez). 13. The view changes where threats come from at least once an act. 14. Warn of off-screen threats with a direction
cue (Gummi, Panzer). 15. Telegraph an attack with a shrinking mark on the foe (Gearbolt). 16. A boss's parts are targets that stage
the fight (Einhander, Silvergun, Uzura). 17. A boss quickens as parts fall (Uzura). 18. A cover opens only to the matching answer
(Buppousou). 19. Return to a set piece's place from another view (Phantom Storm). 20. Mix packed and wide-open spaces (Gearbolt).
21. Bosses scale with what you destroyed (Rez). 22. Damage devolves the ship's form (Rez). 23. Revive before a boss (Orta). 24. Rank
on score, damage taken and boss time (Orta; Ikaruga's seconds). 25. An EX rule challenge a stage (Gummi). 26. Routes branch by
performance (Star Fox 64, Zwei). 27. A cargo condition can fail a medal on a clear (Star Fox's wingmen; inference: casks). 28. One
free-roaming zone (Rez's Area X). 29. Itano swarms mix three missile behaviours. 30. Lens from telephoto to fisheye with distance.
31. Boids plus avoidance and goals. 32. Keep difficulty tunable late (Sin and Punishment, ADEC). 33. Test with high-score players
(Silvergun). 34. Short and replayable. 35. Never unskippable dead time (the Gummi complaint). 36. **No source gave numbers** for foes on
screen or seconds between threats: those are ours to set and measure.

**Not verified:** Orta's boss patterns beyond two; Gearbolt's stage list and any charge mechanic; Star Fox 64's Sector Y and Area 6 as
designed; the Dreadnought's boss; Mizuguchi's GDC 2016 talk.
