# The ships at sea: real counterparts, sci-fi language, retracting masts

Research for Calissa (Art). Not a plan, not binding. Plain names throughout (CLAUDE.md, "Plain names until settled"): sloop, frigate, tanker, destroyer, galleon, mast, sail, hull.
Classes and stats: `src/progress/rail/ships.js`. Today's models: `src/vfx/sloop.js`, `src/vfx/shipclasses.js`.

## 0. How sure this is

The web gave thin results. Fandom wikis answered 402 or 403 to every fetch, so the anime and game design language in section 2 is mostly **recalled, not checked**. Each such line is marked **(recall)**. Facts with a URL are checked. Before an artist copies a shape, open the image links in section 6 and confirm. Ratios marked "mine" are my division of the quoted figures.

## 1. Real silhouettes

An artist needs the long axis first. Ratios are length overall (hull) : beam.

### 1.1 Sloop (fore-and-aft, one mast)
- Definition: one mast, one headsail ahead of it, one mainsail behind it, fore-and-aft rig. Two or more headsails makes it a cutter. A bowsprit is common. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop
- The Jamaica sloop was the narrower, sleeker type; the Bermuda sloop a development of it, and "sharper, lighter, and sleeker than cutters". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_sloop, https://graphsearch.epfl.ch/concept/196264
- Bermuda sloops were most often 22 to 28 ft on deck "with a long bowsprit". No beam figure found. Same Bermuda sloop page.
- Ratio: no checked figure. Use about 3.5:1 on the hull and add the bowsprit on top, so the whole thing reads about 4.5:1. This is my choice, a yacht-like narrow hull; mark it a guess.
- Mast: one, tall, raked a little aft. Boom as long as about 1/3 of the hull. The mast stands about 1.0 to 1.2 hull lengths high.
- Readable in silhouette:
  1. one mast only;
  2. a long bowsprit with a triangular jib hanging from it to the mast head;
  3. a triangular mainsail with a boom hanging out over the stern;
  4. a low, long, open deck with little house;
  5. a fine, slightly raked bow.
- Historic note: before 1900 some sloops carried a square topsail. Not needed.

### 1.2 Frigate (sail age, one gun deck, ship-rigged)
- Classic frigate: guns on one upper deck, the lower deck unarmed, "long sleek hull", "square-rigged". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigate
- French 1777 to 1790 standard: hull about 135 ft (41 m), draught 13 ft, up to 14 knots. Same page.
- The task's "about 45 m" fits a small frigate (36 guns). Endymion class, 40 guns: 159 ft 2 in on the gun deck by 41 ft 2 in beam, so 3.9:1 (mine). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endymion-class_frigate
- USS Constitution, a heavy frigate: 175 ft between perpendiculars, 204 ft overall, beam 43 ft 6 in, so about 4:1 (mine). Three masts; foremast 198 ft, mainmast 220 ft, mizzen 172.5 ft. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constitution
- Mast to hull: the mainmast stands about 1.1 times the hull length high (220 ft on 204 ft), the fore about 1.0, the mizzen about 0.85 (mine, from the figures).
- Readable in silhouette:
  1. three masts, evenly spaced, the middle one tallest;
  2. a row of gun ports along one deck, a pale stripe on the hull (the "Nelson chequer" is later, optional);
  3. a long low hull with a modest sheer, a plain flat stern, no tall castle;
  4. horizontal yards crossing every mast, a fore-and-aft spanker at the stern (the mizzen's lowest sail);
  5. a bowsprit angled up about 20 degrees with a jib or two.

### 1.3 Tanker (oil, modern)
- Layout: accommodation block at the rear, a full-length hull, a pipeline run midships. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_tanker (infobox note).
- Newer tankers are double-hulled. Same page.
- The largest ships are 380 m (TI class) to over 400 m. Same page. Typical tanker L:B is about 5.5:1 to 6.5:1 for the very large ones (recall, from the common 380 m x 68 m class; not fetched).
- Bulbous bow: a bulb just under the waterline, best on heavy or fast ships, such as supertankers. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulbous_bow
- Readable in silhouette:
  1. all the height is at the stern: a boxy block of 4 to 6 decks with a funnel on top;
  2. a very long, flat, low deck, with almost nothing above it;
  3. a pipe manifold, a row of short pipes and a gantry across the deck at midships; a catwalk along the centreline from bow to house;
  4. a bulb under the bow that sticks forward at the waterline; a blunt, full bow;
  5. a flat-sided hull, slab ends, a full square stern with a rudder post.
- A mast: tankers carry one short mast on the house and one small mast at the bow. Both are tiny, which is why retracting is cheap.

### 1.4 Destroyer (modern)
- Arleigh Burke: 154 to 155 m long, 20 m beam, so 7.7:1 (mine). The task's 9:1 is the older Spruance and the Type 45 range; use 8:1 as a middle. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arleigh_Burke-class_destroyer
- Wide flaring bow for seakeeping. A 5-inch gun on the forward deck. VLS in two modules (29 and 61 cells, or 32 and 64), one forward of the bridge, one aft. Source: same page.
- Superstructure: angled faces to cut radar return; a raked tripod mast; a helicopter deck at the stern, hangars on later flights. Same page.
- Readable in silhouette:
  1. a long, sharp, raked bow with the hull sweeping up, a high forecastle;
  2. one gun turret forward, a small wedge, low, on the centreline;
  3. a slab-sided central superstructure, one block, with a mast on top and angled planes;
  4. low aft deck: helicopter pad, one more VLS block just aft of the superstructure;
  5. a pair of short stacks or a single raked one, merged into the block.
- Hull shape from the top: a long thin blade, widest at about 60 percent aft of the bow, a cut-off transom.

### 1.5 Galleon (16th and 17th century)
- Longer, lower, narrower than a carrack. A prominent squared stern. A long beakhead under the forecastle. Three or four masts: fore and main square, mizzen lateen, often a fourth lateen "bonaventure". Usually under 500 tons, some about 1000 to 2000. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleon
- Ratio: no checked figure. The task's 4:1 is a good middle for a late galleon (recall that a Spanish galleon's keel to beam was about 3:1, giving a stubbier look; use 3.5 to 4:1). Mark as a guess.
- Readable in silhouette:
  1. a tall stern castle, stepped back in two or three decks, the highest point of the hull;
  2. a smaller forecastle, lower than the stern, with a beak (the beakhead) reaching out forward and low;
  3. a strong sheer: the deck dips in the middle (the waist) and rises at both ends;
  4. a lateen mizzen: a long diagonal yard, the one triangular sail in a field of squares;
  5. tumblehome: the hull narrows above the waterline, so the top view is a fat egg and the deck a narrow one.

## 2. The sci-fi references

All design lines here are **(recall)** unless a URL is given. See section 6 for image searches.

### 2.1 Outlaw Star (Takehiko Ito's manga, Sunrise 1998)
- Checked: the XGP15A-II is a prototype stolen from the Kei Pirates; only flyable with Melfina, a bio-android; two large grappler arms normally folded on the underside; arms carry a machine gun and a ship-sized combat axe; drives are the Munchausen Drive and a Sub-Ether Drive. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_Star, https://outlawstar.fandom.com/wiki/Outlaw_Star_(ship)?oldid=7728 (a search result, not fetched in full).
- Checked: Shoji Kawamori (Macross) is credited with designing the Outlaw Star itself, Junya Ishigaki with the show's vehicles. Source: the Wikipedia page above. (A search found it unconfirmed on the fan wiki.)
- Paint: one wiki says red and blue, originally all green, a large star decal on the hull. Another says Gene had it repainted red. Fan wikis disagree.
- Shape grammar (recall): a stubby, deep, chunky hull; hard planar plates, not curves; a blunt nose with a sharp keel line; big rear engine pods; the arms hide in recesses and unfold outward and down. Colour blocks of white, red, blue with dark grey mechanical joints. Thruster glow is white-blue.
- Lesson for us: **parts that hide inside a clean hull and then deploy**. This is the retraction grammar we want. The grappler arms are the model for the mount arms and the mast.

### 2.2 Tenchi Muyo (Jurai ships, Ryo-Ohki)
- Checked: treeships carry a living tree in the core, descended from the Chousin Tsunami. The wooden hull is a "limiter" for the tree's power. There is no standard layout; each ship's shape follows its personality and its owner. Funaho can sit inside the frame of a standard starship as a disguise. Source: https://tenchi.fandom.com/wiki/Juraian_Treeships (search result text).
- Checked: Light Hawk Wings are made by royal-tree ships; Funaho and Mizuho together make a flower-pattern wing. Same page and https://tenchi.fandom.com/wiki/Mizuho.
- Checked: Ryo-Ohki is a grey cabbit that becomes a ship; Washu-made, not a treeship; used gems. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenchi_Muyo!_Ryo-Ohki
- Shape grammar (recall): Jurai ships are slim, long and curved, like a seed pod or a leaf; no sharp planes, no visible guns; colours of pale wood, cream and white with green; the Light Hawk Wings are three or more curved fins of pale gold-green light that unfold from the hull in a fan. Ryo-Ohki in ship form is a dark, crystalline, angular body with a bright central gem; crystal edges are faceted.
- Lesson for us: **light is the sail**. Wings of light fan out from a clean hull. The hull has few panel lines. A "core" glows in the middle and spreads colour outward.

### 2.3 Skies of Arcadia (Sega, 2000)
- Checked: Little Jack is Drachma's ship, an old fishing ship fortified against battleships. The Delphinus is a Valuan prototype battleship carrying a Moonstone Cannon. Valuan ships are iron-clad, armour-plated, larger than most; flagships share one body with detail changes, with "blade things" on the deck. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skies_of_Arcadia, https://art.cleberg.net/post/stevolteon/The-Cygnus-620451156 (search snippet), https://skiesofarcadia.fandom.com/wiki/Valuan_Armada?oldid=5994 (search snippet).
- Checked: Albatross looks like a traditional sailing ship with five broadside cannons a side. Source: https://academickids.com/encyclopedia/index.php/Battle_of_Soltis (search snippet).
- Shape grammar (recall): the Blue Rogues' ships are sea ships lifted into the air: a wooden or painted hull, with big fins or wings, a propeller or two, and a sail or sail-like fin. The Delphinus is white and blue with a long needle bow and a large rear fin. Valuan ships are dark grey and purple, with an angular hull, a ram and spikes, and large engine rings. Moonstones glow in colour at the engine.
- Lesson for us: **a sea hull with air fittings**. Keep the keel, deck, rail, and add wings, fins and a propeller. Two factions give two palettes: rogue (painted, warm) and armoured (dark, spiked).

### 2.4 Rogue Galaxy (Level-5, 2005)
- Checked: the Dorgenark is an intergalactic pirate ship; a reviewer says it looks like an 1800s schooner, with sails and a Jolly Roger; a database calls it a space galleon. Sources: https://www.worthplaying.com/article/2007/3/19/reviews/40609-ps2-review-rogue-galaxy/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Galaxy
- Not found: sail mechanics, solar sails. The claim that its sails are solar sails is unverified. Pinterest has "Dorgenark Ship Art" (concept art): https://www.pinterest.com/pin/320600067195922487/
- Shape grammar (recall): a wooden-looking galleon hull with a tall stern, masts and sails, flying through space; engine glow at the stern; cel-shaded.
- Lesson for us: the **literal sailing ship in space**. The sails stay as sails. Our galleon can do the same: keep the full rig, with light in the cloth.

### 2.5 Shared grammar (what the four have in common)
1. A hull that reads as a boat first, with a few non-boat parts.
2. One strong colour block scheme per ship, with dark grey joints.
3. A single bright source (engine, gem, core) that the eye goes to.
4. Parts that deploy from a closed form (arms, wings, fins).
5. Wing or sail shapes made of light or thin cloth, not solid.

## 3. Fusion brief per class

Budget: a few hundred to about 3000 triangles a ship, one shared material with a panel-line and emissive shader. Master plan for the shader: a panel-line mask (second UV or vertex colour), an emissive mask (vertex alpha), a base colour from a 4-colour palette per class. The clay pot look is dropped or kept only as the glaze colours, with no pot belly. The owner allowed branching out.

Common parts (build once, reuse):
- **Spine.** A hull-length keel line. A slot in the deck where the mast folds in. Panel line along its edge.
- **Mast unit.** A mast of 2 or 3 telescoping segments plus a pivot foot. Sails are vanes on it.
- **Vane.** A flat triangle or quad (2 to 8 triangles) with an emissive edge and a translucent fill. Scales along the mast to fold.
- **Core.** A faceted gem (8 to 20 triangles) set in the hull's middle, visible from above through a window. Its colour is the ship's feeling or the form (Astral/Umbral).
- **Arms.** Two hinged blocks on the underside (the Outlaw Star's grapplers), used by the mounts.
- **Engine pods.** Two cylinders aft with a glow disc.

### 3.1 Sloop: "the dinghy-yacht" (Outlaw Star meets a Bermuda sloop)
- Real read: one mast, a bowsprit with a jib, a boom with a mainsail, a long narrow hull. L:B about 4:1 including bowsprit.
- Sci-fi dress: the jib is a triangular vane, the main a bigger one. Both emissive on the edge only. The bowsprit is a slender lance (it can carry a mount). Hull is a narrow wedge, two pods aft, a gem at the base of the mast (the "step"). Palette: white, red, a dark blue joint, white-blue glow (Outlaw Star's).
- Top view: a long arrowhead with a short bar (the boom) at the stern end. Side view: one tall triangle plus one small triangle ahead.
- Triangles: hull 250, mast 60, vanes 20, pods 80, gem 20; about 450.

### 3.2 Frigate: "the three-mast cutter of light"
- Real read: three masts, the middle tallest, yards crossing, a gun-port stripe, a long low hull. L:B about 4:1.
- Sci-fi dress: the yards become thin horizontal bars; the sails between are square vanes of translucent cloth with a bright foot-edge. Gun ports are 8 to 12 small emissive slits along each side. A bowsprit and a jib vane. A small fin on each side aft (a Delphinus wing). One pod aft under the stern gallery.
- Top view: a long oval with three short crossbars at 25, 50 and 75 percent. Side view: three towers of squares, graded in height.
- Palette: cream hull, a dark-wood or deep-teal stripe, gold trim, a green-gold glow (Jurai wood).
- Triangles: hull 500, three masts 150, nine vanes 90, ports 100; about 900.

### 3.3 Tanker: "the flat barge with a tower"
- Real read: the house aft, a flat deck, a manifold midships, a bulb at the bow. L:B about 6:1.
- Sci-fi dress: the house becomes a faceted tower with a crystal in the top (the "tree core" borrowed, as the Lachryma crude tank's gem). The deck holds a row of glowing tank domes (the crude) and a pipe gantry at midships. The mast is one short lattice on the tower top with a small radar vane. The bulb at the bow is a glowing teardrop.
- Top view: a long rectangle with a box at the aft end and a row of circles. Side view: a flat line, one tower at the stern, a small bump at the bow.
- Palette: oxide red hull, dark grey deck, a yellow warning stripe, amber glow (crude).
- Triangles: hull 300, house 200, tanks 150, pipes 100, bulb 40; about 800.
- The tanker never dives (ships.js `dive: false`), so it only retracts for a barrel roll, if it rolls at all. Check with the rail's code.

### 3.4 Destroyer: "the blade"
- Real read: a raked bow, a gun forward, a single superstructure block with an angled mast, a flat aft deck. L:B 8:1 to 9:1.
- Sci-fi dress: a razor hull with panel lines like a Macross fighter. A small turret forward, a row of VLS hatches that glow when firing. The superstructure is a wedge with a mast that is a flat blade, folding flat on the roof. Engine pods are big and square, aft.
- Top view: a long thin dart, widest 60 percent back. Side view: a rising bow, a wedge in the middle, flat tail.
- Palette: grey-blue hull, a dark navy deck, a hot red-orange accent (it is the fastest, the fragile hunter), blue-white engine glow.
- Triangles: hull 350, block 150, turret 60, mast 40, pods 100, VLS 80; about 800.

### 3.5 Galleon: "the Dorgenark"
- Real read: a tall stern castle, a lower forecastle, a beakhead, a lateen mizzen, a strong sheer. L:B 3.5:1 to 4:1.
- Sci-fi dress: keep the full sailing rig, and make it big. Square vanes fore and main, a lateen vane aft, a long beak that is a prow-lance. The stern castle holds the engine ring (Skies of Arcadia): a glowing circle on its back. A skull or star emblem (a star, not a lore symbol) on the stern. A keel fin under the hull.
- Top view: a fat egg with a narrow deck, a beak out the front. Side view: a high stern, a low waist, a tall sail stack.
- Palette: deep brown-red hull, gold trim, white sails with a coloured edge, a violet-gold glow.
- Triangles: hull 700, castles 400, masts 200, vanes 200, beak 60, ring 100; about 1700.
- The galleon also does not dive (`dive: false`).

- Test each class from the side and the top as a **black silhouette** at 64 px tall. If you cannot name the class, change the silhouette, not the colour.
- Keep every ship's length on the rail the same screen size; the class shows in proportion, not scale (ships.js `hurtbox` handles size).

## 4. Mast retraction

### 4.1 In real life
- **Tabernacle mast.** A deck bracket with a pivot bolt; the mast swings down about 90 degrees, to pass under a bridge or go on a trailer. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_(sailing) (nomenclature section). One hinge, no parts to unscrew; this is the simplest model.
- **Telescoping masts.** Submarine photonics masts rise "similar to a telescoping antenna" and can sit entirely inside the sail fairing. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photonics_mast. A hoist-and-lower patent shows a rotatable mast raised by jacks: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4436051
- **Lowering for canals.** Barges and small yachts strike the mast in the same way: a counterweighted pivot or a gin pole. (recall)
- **Square-rig sail age.** Topgallant masts were "struck" (lowered) in a gale: the upper mast slid down alongside the lower one. This is a real, historic telescoping. (recall)

### 4.2 In fiction
- **Macross Valkyrie.** Fighter, Gerwalk and Battroid modes: panels slide and rotate in a clear sequence, a leg folds, an arm unfolds, the form never loses its centre line. Shoji Kawamori also designed the Outlaw Star (see 2.1). (recall for the mechanics; the credit is checked.)
- **Outlaw Star's grappler arms.** Fold flat to the underside, unfold sideways. (checked: section 2.1)
- **Skies of Arcadia sails.** Whether the sails fold on screen is not confirmed by my sources. (recall: ships have sail-like fins that unfurl on entering a battle.) Treat as unverified.
- **Dorgenark sails.** Not confirmed.
- **Jurai Light Hawk Wings.** Appear and vanish as light, with no mechanics (recall). Good for the vanes: a vane can dissolve to its edge line.

### 4.3 Recommendations
The whole fold must read in the first third of its time, because a viewer reads the **start** pose and the **end** pose. Use an ease-in-out with a tiny overshoot at the end (about 5 percent), and a 1-frame anticipation (the mast leans 3 degrees the wrong way) only on the 0.5 s fold.

**Barrel roll, 0.3 s (the ship rolls 360 degrees; the mast must not hit the water or the camera).**
- Do: the vanes first close like a fan or a book (0 to 0.1 s), then the mast telescopes down (0.05 to 0.25 s), the last 0.05 s is a settle. Reverse in the last 0.1 s of the roll so the mast is out when the roll ends. In total the mast is down for about 0.2 s.
- Why: a mast sticking up during a roll sweeps a wide arc and clips the lane. Telescoping changes the length rather than the angle, so the silhouette stays inside the hull's circle.
- Per class:
  - sloop: 2-segment telescope, jib vane furls toward the bowsprit;
  - frigate: topmasts strike (telescope), yards pivot to lie fore-and-aft (a 90-degree turn, quick to read);
  - tanker: the short mast drops into the house; the roll is probably not allowed for it, so skip;
  - destroyer: the blade mast folds flat backward on a hinge (a tabernacle), 70 degrees in 0.15 s;
  - galleon: only the tall topgallants strike; the lower masts stay (a galleon is slow and does not roll in the lanes). If it must, use the frigate rule.

**Form flip, 0.5 s (Astral to Umbral, the ship dives under the sea).**
- Do, in this order: 0.00 to 0.15 s the vanes dim and fold; 0.10 to 0.35 s the mast telescopes or hinges into the spine slot; 0.30 to 0.45 s a hatch panel slides over the slot and its panel line lights up; 0.45 to 0.50 s the hull colour and core colour switch to the new form. The ship noses down only after the hatch shuts.
- Umbral look: a smooth sealed hull, a periscope-like stub (a photonics mast, a short stalk) may stay up as the one sensor. This gives a real-world anchor and a visible difference between the forms.
- Rising again: reverse, but 0.1 s faster; going up should feel eager, going down should feel sealed.
- Per class:
  - sloop: mast telescopes into a deck slot; a hatch closes; a short stub stays;
  - frigate: three masts strike to the spine in sequence, aft to fore, 0.05 s apart (a ripple that reads as mechanical);
  - destroyer: the mast folds back and the VLS hatches close in a ripple;
  - tanker and galleon: do not dive; no flip.

### 4.4 Implementation notes for the art
- Build the mast as a **chain of boxes** (segments) with a pivot at its foot, so the same rig can telescope (scale along y, segment offsets) or hinge (rotate about the foot). One animation parameter `u` from 0 (out) to 1 (stowed) drives both. Per-class easing curves live in data.
- The vane's size is `1 - u` along its mast; at `u = 1` its scale is 0 and it is hidden, so it costs no triangles in the stow.
- Panel-line glow on the hatch lights on the **last** 20 percent of the fold, so the glow marks the end.
- Check the pose from the front and the side with other tools off (CLAUDE.md, Animation). Pose audit: `scripts/poseaudit.mjs` is the Courier's, not the ships'; a ship's check is a headless shot of `u = 0, 0.5, 1`.
