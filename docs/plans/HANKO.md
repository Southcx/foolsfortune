# The Pneuka Jar as a seal: sealing into a cradle (names Espada's, proposed: `docs/LORE.md`, "The Pneuka Jar as hanko")

Kept by Dovina (the rules, the ledger, the progression). Names and lore are Espada's; the Jar's seal face and each cradle's look are
Calissa's; the form change and a body's controller are Petra's; the stamp's sound is Wanda's. **Ruled by the owner, 2026-10-17 (section 6).**

> "I see the Pneuka Jar as a form of hanko... the Pneuka Jar gets slotted into things like a cartridge to make them work. Things like
> larger vessels or machines, the shrine gates, the ships for the crossings." (the owner, 2026-10-17, to Calissa)

## 0. The idea in one line

**Your Pneuka Jar is your seal and your cartridge.** At a **cradle** (a receptacle built into a machine, a gate, a ship's helm) the
Courier folds back into the Jar and **seals** into it: the Jar's base stamps your mark, and the thing wakes and runs as you, until you
leave it.

## 1. Why it is already true in the fiction

- The Jar "can house Players, people outside the game" (LORE.md). A shell that holds a player and is slotted into a machine to make it
  run is a **game cartridge**. The meta joke is the lore itself (the System register).
- A **hanko** is the seal that makes a document true: your mark, carved once, pressed to authorise. The Jar's base carries **your
  seal**, the Comb's stamp (Calissa's hex Comb, kept as the hanko signature, OVERLAY.md 15.6). Sealing a cradle is pressing your
  hanko: the machine accepts you as its owner.
- The ships are already "forms of the Vessoul". Sealing into a ship's helm makes that literal: the hull becomes your body.
- The god hand is "the same will reaching out while the body rests as the jar". Sealing is the other half of that: the jar does not
  rest, it **drives**.

## 2. Prior art, and what each gives

- **Mega Man Battle Network's jack-in** ("Jack in! Execute!"): every machine in the world has a port, and plugging in enters it. The
  cyber register exactly; taken: any machine may have a cradle, and sealing in is a named, ceremonial act.
- **Breath of the Wild's Sheikah Slate pedestals**: the one object you carry wakes a shrine or a tower when set into it; taken: the
  Shrines' gates and the fast-travel network are woken by sealing.
- **Metroid's Morph Ball sockets**: the hero's own body, small, is the key; taken: the Courier becomes the Jar to fit.
- **Kirby Planet Robobot's armour, Mario Odyssey's capture**: a bigger body piloted with its own moves; taken: the larger vessels.
- **The Japanese seal culture**: a registered seal (jitsuin) for what matters, a document needing two seals for two parties; taken:
  a cradle may want two seals (co-op), and your seal is registered once.

## 3. The act

1. **F at a cradle.** The Courier's body folds into the Pneuka Jar (the god hand's form change, the same clip family), the Jar drops
   into the cradle and turns a quarter, and the seal **stamps**: your Comb imprint burns into the cradle's face in your window colour.
   One beat, always the same: a ceremony you learn to love, never a load screen (the stamp is a hero moment: OVERLAY.md 1, rule 3).
2. **The thing wakes as you.** It wears your seal while you are in it. The overlay shows what it is (a tag: its name, its draw on
   your pool, its own hull if it has one).
3. **F again unseals.** The Jar lifts out and unfolds into the Courier beside it. The seal's imprint stays on the cradle, dim: you have
   been here.

## 4. Three kinds of cradle

| kind | what sealing does | examples | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a threshold** | a stamp, at once: it opens, and remembers your seal | a Shrine's gate (attunes it to the fast-travel network), a sealed door, a ruin's lock | none; the first seal of each is a ledger first |
| **an engine** | it runs while you are sealed in, on your pool | a lift, a pump, the Weir's sluice, a kiln, a forge, a drawbridge | a draw on the pool a real second, shown on its tag (a modifier with a visible source, never a base change) |
| **a body** | you drive it: its own moves, its own hull | **the ships** (the helm's cradle at the pier begins a crossing), a clay golem, a dredge, a diving bell | the pool is its hull (as the board's rules make the pool the skiff's); empty, the body fails and the Jar is thrown clear |

- **The core movement is untouched.** A body is its own controller, as the skiff is; unsealing restores the Courier exactly.
- **One seal at a time.** While sealed, the Courier is the Jar in the cradle: it can be struck if the cradle is (an engine under
  attack is a defend-the-point beat), and a broken body throws the Jar clear, cracked.
- **Two seals.** Some cradles take two Jars (a great gate, a galleon's twin helms): a co-op cradle, opened by a guest or a sibling
  sealing beside you. The windows stay private (the owner, 2026-10-16); a seal is the one thing of yours others see.

## 5. What it is worth (progression and the ledger)

- **Seals are a record of where you have been.** `seal.<cradle>` is a first in the ledger; the Codex shows a **Seal Book** (every imprint
  you have pressed, by island, in its dress). Achievements read it (count ways: thresholds sealed per island; feat ways: a body driven
  through a set piece unbroken).
- **The Shrines' network becomes the Seal Book's spine**: a Shrine you have sealed is one you can travel to. No other unlock gate.
- **Bodies are found, never bought**: a golem in a ruin, a diving bell at the Weir. The law holds (arts by achievement); a body is a
  place's thing, not an art.
- **No cubes.** Sealing costs the pool or nothing. The economy is untouched; the pool is the substrate (base change bad).
- **Your seal is chosen once, at character creation, and never changed** (the owner, 2026-10-17: "Recarving your seal would be like
  making a new account. I think people should choose their stamp during character creation and take it seriously"). Not a sink. The
  choice is made of three things, each from real seal-carving:
  1. **The name**: two or three syllables (a cell of the Comb each, Espada's hex rules), typed and spelt in neuralese's sounds.
  2. **The outline**: round (maru), square (kaku), oval, or gourd: the four common hanko shapes.
  3. **The carving**: relief, the name standing in ink (shubun, red letters), or intaglio, the name cut into the ink (hakubun, white).
  The ink is the window colour. It is kept in a save section of scope `settings`, so it survives a new build as a name would; progress
  resets around it.

### Espada's names and lore (2026-10-17, proposed)
- **a cradle** (neutral to every island; not "saggar", which would say Anagami), **sealing / to seal in / to unseal** (not "press": the
  Athanor's), **your seal** (the Jar's base; beside **an attribute's seal**, the potter's chop on the press's kerb: always qualified),
  **an imprint** (what a seal leaves; not "print": the trail map's), **the Seal Book** (the Codex shelf).
- **The Seal Book works both ways, as a pilgrim's goshuinchō**: your seal presses an imprint into the cradle, and the cradle stamps
  its own seal back into your book. So the book is a collection of the places' seals, each in its island's dress (Dovina takes it: the
  ledger's `seal.<cradle>` first records both).
- **Why a Jar fits**: the jar is the oldest vessel people made, and every island precipitates out of the one Emocean, so every island's
  machines carry a jar-sized hollow left for a will to fill. Who built the first cradle is left blank.
